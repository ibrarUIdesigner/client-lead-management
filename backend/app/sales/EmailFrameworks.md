# Email Frameworks Playbook

Guidelines and structural frameworks for crafting high-converting, human cold outreach.

## 1. Core Principles & Strict Rules

- **Word Count**: Keep initial outreach strictly between 50 and 90 words. Short emails get read and replied to.
- **Tone**: Calm, peer-to-peer, respectful, specific. Write like a skilled designer speaking to a fellow business owner.
- **Banned Buzzwords & AI Tropes**:
  - Never use: "I hope this email finds you well", "In today's fast-paced digital world", "game-changer", "synergy", "cutting-edge", "unleash", "streamline", "leverage", "delve", "robust", "transformative", "bespoke".
  - Never flatter insincerely ("I was blown away by your stunning website").
  - Never invent problems not verified in the audit or research data.
- **Single Clear Call-to-Action (CTA)**: Ask a single low-friction, interest-based question (e.g., "Would you be open to taking a look?", "Would it be helpful if I sent the preview link over?").
- **Signature**: Always conclude with sender name, portfolio link, WhatsApp, and contact email.

## 2. Frameworks

### Framework 1: Mockup-Led
**When to Use**: A custom homepage concept has already been planned or generated for this lead.
**Structure**:
1. Casual opening identifying their business.
2. Direct observation of an opportunity to showcase their services or improve conversion.
3. Concrete offer: Mention you put together a quick homepage concept specifically for them.
4. Low-friction CTA: Ask if they'd like to see it.

```text
Hi {{first_name|there}},

I noticed {{business_name}} has an active reputation in {{city}}, but your current website doesn't make {{primary_service_or_booking}} immediately obvious to visitors on mobile.

I put together a quick visual concept for a refreshed homepage showing how that could look with a dedicated booking flow. 

Would you be open to taking a look if I send the preview link over?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

### Framework 2: Audit-Led
**When to Use**: Lead has a live website with verified high-impact UX, mobile, or performance friction points.
**Structure**:
1. Specific observation mentioning 1 or 2 concrete technical findings (e.g. mobile navigation overflow, 4s+ LCP, missing primary CTA).
2. Explain the business impact (visitors bouncing before calling or submitting an inquiry).
3. Value proposition: Offer to resolve those specific blockers for a clear settled price.
4. CTA: Interest-based close.

```text
Hi {{first_name|there}},

I was looking at {{business_name}}'s website earlier. I noticed the largest content takes over 4 seconds to load on mobile phones, and the main phone button gets pushed below the fold.

When customers are looking for a {{industry_label}} in {{city}}, that friction often leads them to tap back and call the next listing.

I specialize in fixing these conversion bottlenecks. Would you like me to share a couple of quick ideas on how to smooth this out?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

---

### Framework 3: Research-Led
**When to Use**: Lead has strong local standing, recent expansion, active reviews, or a competitor gap identified via research.
**Structure**:
1. Compliment a real, verified milestone (e.g., strong review count or recent service addition).
2. Contrast with a digital gap (e.g., competitors offering instant booking or project showcase while their site lacks it).
3. Offer a modern solution tailored to that market reality.
4. CTA.

```text
Hi {{first_name|there}},

Saw that {{business_name}} has built a solid track record in {{city}} with great customer reviews.

One thing I noticed while researching {{industry_label}} in the area is that several local competitors now offer online appointment requests, while your site still relies purely on manual calls.

Adding a direct appointment section would capture visitors outside business hours. Would you be interested in seeing how we could set that up?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

---

### Framework 4: Opportunity-Led (No Website)
**When to Use**: Business operates with no discovered website, or has only an unmaintained social link.
**Structure**:
1. Acknowledge their existing business presence (Google listing / reviews).
2. Frame the lost customer opportunity: prospective clients searching for their specific services online who cannot find verified details or request a quote.
3. Direct proposition: build a professional, clean homepage with clear contact methods.
4. Fixed price mention ($850) and low-friction CTA.

```text
Hi {{first_name|there}},

I came across {{business_name}} while looking at {{industry_label}} providers in {{city}}. You have great customer feedback, but I noticed you don't have an official website listed.

A lot of potential customers search online before hiring a {{industry_label}}, and having a clean, single-page site makes it easy for them to verify your services and get in touch.

I can build a complete, modern homepage for {{business_name}} for a fixed $850. Would you be interested in seeing a quick draft?

Best,
{{sender_name}}
```

---

### Framework 5: Problem + Solution (Direct)
**When to Use**: Clear, isolated high-friction problem (e.g., missing booking form or missing portfolio).
**Structure**:
1. Plain statement of what was noticed.
2. The exact solution proposed.
3. Low-pressure CTA.
